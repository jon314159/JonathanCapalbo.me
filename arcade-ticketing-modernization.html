Arcade ticketing

For a fictional arcade, I designed a way for staff to report a problem, assign an owner and check the fix before closing the ticket. I wrote the requirements and planned tests for five types of issues, including machine repairs and refunds.

Who owns the next step?

In this scenario, a staff member can report a problem by phone, on paper, in a message or in person. The next shift has to piece together who took the report, how urgent it is and whether anyone fixed it.

I proposed one ticket for broken machines, game-card issues, refunds, prize-counter problems and facility incidents at a single location. The shift lead would assign each ticket to a named owner. Refunds would also need a manager’s approval.

I wrote the acceptance criteria and rollout plan. The project ended at planning, before software configuration, testing or measurement.

Who can approve a refund?

The ticket stays In Progress while an authorized manager other than the requester decides. Staff can fulfill a refund up to the approved amount. If the manager declines, staff record the reason, tell the customer and resolve the ticket as declined.

Refund decision criteria

Who follows up while work is on hold?

A ticket waiting for parts, a vendor or a customer keeps its owner. The owner records what they need, the next step and a review date. The shift lead checks overdue tickets. Work returns to In Progress when it can resume.

Pending criteria

When does a ticket leave the backlog?

A Resolved ticket still counts as open. The shift lead can close it after verifying the fix, or return it to In Progress if the check fails. If the issue comes back after closure, staff open a new ticket and link it to the original.

Read the process as text

In the fictional arcade, staff report problems in conversation, on paper, by phone or in messages. Priority and ownership depend on who hears the report. Repair updates and refund decisions can end up in different places.

In the proposed process, staff address immediate hazards first and create a New ticket when it is safe. The shift lead confirms impact and priority (Triaged), then assigns a team and a named owner (Assigned). If the route is unclear, the shift lead keeps responsibility.

The owner records actions while the ticket is In Progress. A refund stays In Progress while a manager decides whether to approve it. If work is blocked, the owner can mark it Pending, record why, set the next action and review date, then return it to In Progress when work resumes.

The owner adds action notes and a resolution code to mark a ticket Resolved. If verification fails, the shift lead records why and returns it to the same owner as In Progress. If the check passes, the ticket can be Closed with the verifier’s name and time. Staff keep the closed history and link any later recurrence to it in a new ticket.
