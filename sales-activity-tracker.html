At Comcast/Xfinity

Why the monthly total came up short

I used my Excel tracker to check my progress toward goals. When I reviewed the monthly formulas, I found they skipped some days. I corrected them so those days counted toward the total.

In the public workbook, formulas pull each day’s figures into one row of Monthly Summary. You can compare that row with the daily worksheet and check exactly what went into the monthly total.

Design decisions

How I made the workbook easier to check

The daily sheets share the same fields so I can compare one day with another and trace a monthly figure back to its source.

Workbook design decisions, their implementation, and their operational value
DecisionWorkbook implementationWhy it matters
Set goals in one placeThe Settings sheet holds the product names, goals, target days, and point values.Change a goal once to update the relevant comparisons.
Use the same fields each dayThirty-one matching tables use validation for product, outcome, follow-up, and units.Product names and outcomes match across all 31 sheets.
Keep a daily summaryFormulas pull the daily figures into Monthly Summary. The dashboard uses those totals.You can check one day’s totals without editing its entries.
Flag days below goalIF, IFERROR, SUMIF, and COUNTIF calculate totals and goal status. Conditional formatting highlights the results.Below-goal days and follow-up activity are easier to find.

Judgment and trade-off

Why use 31 daily tabs?

A daily tab puts the entries beside their totals, so I can check one day without filtering a month of records.

Maintenance trade-offIf I change a daily formula, I have to update each sheet that uses it and recheck the monthly totals.

Recommendation

What I’d check before changing the process

I’d first ask whether the daily and monthly goals were set independently on purpose. Then I’d compare activity counts and completed units on the 17 days that fell short.

The workbook can show which days to investigate. To explain why they fell short, I’d also need to know about staffing and demand on those days.
