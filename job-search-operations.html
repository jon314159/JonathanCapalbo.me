I adapted Mads Lorentzen’s AI Job Search to keep track of which résumé I’d checked, whether an employer received my application and when I last followed up.

What I changed

Lorentzen wrote the original framework and commands. I maintain my own version in a private Git fork. It uses Python to work with JSON and CSV records and check résumé PDFs. AI assistants help me coordinate the work.

Recheck a résumé after an edit

I tied each review to the exact résumé and supporting material I checked. If I change a claim or rebuild the PDF, the tracker flags the affected checks for another review.

Track interrupted submissions

A portal timeout can mean the application went through and the receipt never appeared. I added an “uncertain” status that requires me to check what happened before trying again. I only mark the role as applied once I have confirmation.

Count follow-ups when I send them

I keep drafts separate from messages I’ve sent. Reminders now use the last actual contact date. I flagged older entries for review wherever I couldn’t tell whether I’d sent the message.

How I handle an application

I use an AI assistant and local tools to find openings and prepare materials. I review the application and decide whether to send it.

Application steps

  1. Set the search

    I give the assistant my preferences and limits for this request.

    ↓

  2. Coordinate the search

    The assistant asks for missing details, passes the request to my local tools and brings back the results.

    ↓

  3. Check the openings

    My tools standardize listings, look for duplicates and save copies of the postings. Before ranking a role, they check its availability on the employer’s site or application portal and look for evidence that it meets my requirements.

    ↓

  4. Prepare the materials

    The workflow evaluates the role and helps draft and revise the application. I check the résumé’s claims against my experience.

    ↓

  5. Approve the application

    I review the documents, answers, consent requests and certifications before approving submission.

    ↓

  6. Complete the portal steps

    The browser carries out the steps I approved. I take over when the portal needs a manual action or an account check.

    ↓

  7. Check for a receipt

    I look for confirmation tied to the same opening and account. If the portal leaves the outcome unclear, I check its status before any retry.

    ↓

  8. Update the tracker

    I match the opening to its tracker row and saved files, then preview the update before recording the status and documents.

What I check before updating the tracker

Do two listings refer to the same job?

I compare the postings before combining them. If I can confirm a match, I keep both sources and note any conflicting details. When the match is unclear, I keep the records separate.

Is the job still open?

I save a copy of the posting with its source and the time I checked it. If the site blocks the request or returns an unreadable page, I leave the status unresolved until I can verify it with the employer or its application portal.

Is this the résumé I reviewed?

I check the PDF’s page count and extracted text, look at its layout and verify its claims. When the document or the evidence behind a claim changes, I repeat the affected checks before using it.

Did the employer receive it?

I look for confirmation on the same job and account. If I only have a prepared résumé or a record of clicking Submit, I check the portal before changing the application status. I preview the tracker update before saving.

What I know

Tracker status

What happens next

The portal confirms receipt

I save the receipt, review the tracker update and mark the application confirmed.

The result is still unclear

I keep the status uncertain and pause until I can verify what happened with the employer or its portal.

The portal confirms it received nothing

I record that evidence before resetting the status. Another submission requires my approval.

What the regression tests covered

On September 19, 2026, three regression tests passed using temporary sample records. One changed the résumé source, PDF, verification rules and claim text one at a time. Each change invalidated the earlier check. The other two tests verified that resetting an uncertain attempt requires evidence and that recording a confirmed application requires proof of receipt.

On September 28, 2026, follow-up tests passed on sample messages. Drafts didn’t count as attempts, two records of the same sent message counted once, and editing a tracker entry preserved the original contact date.

Both runs tested selected rules locally. A full application through a live portal was outside their scope.

I maintain the code and its change history privately, along with my credentials and application records. I built these changes for my own search. I haven’t measured time savings or hiring outcomes.
