Academic team project · Tableau

Chicago food inspections

I built four Tableau dashboards to see where Chicago food inspections failed and how that changed from 2019 to 2025. I put inspection counts beside the rates so readers could tell whether a high percentage represented a handful of visits or thousands.

Open in Tableau Public Read the findings Download workbook (5.7 MB)
Team and data source

I worked on this class project with Camerun Hannah, Mostafa Moawad and Yovanny Moscoso Huaman. I built all four dashboards from nineteen worksheets. The name under each dashboard heading identifies the teammate assigned to present it.

The October 17, 2026 data snapshot covers 123,673 inspections from 2019 through 2025, including 103,614 with an evaluated outcome.

Main finding

The annual failure rate fell from 24.9% to 21.1% between 2023 and 2025.

That’s a drop of about 3.8 percentage points among evaluated inspections. Both the number of visits and the mix of facilities inspected can change from year to year.

Across all seven years, 23,843 of 103,614 evaluated inspections failed, giving an overall failure rate of 23.0%.

Evaluated inspections have a Pass, Pass w/ Conditions or Fail result. These counts describe inspection visits, including repeat visits to the same establishment.

Inspection context

Failure rates varied by reason for inspection

Complaint inspections had a 37.4% failure rate, compared with 24.5% for routine canvass inspections and 11.6% for re-inspections.

A reported concern triggers a complaint inspection. That difference in how visits begin makes the rates hard to compare directly.

Source and preparation

How I calculated the failure rate

City of Chicago Food Inspections is the public source. The October 17, 2026 snapshot contains 317,175 rows. I kept the 123,673 inspections from the seven complete calendar years 2019 through 2025.

Failure-rate calculation
I divided the number of failures by the number of visits with a Pass, Pass w/ Conditions or Fail result. When a filter changes, Tableau repeats that calculation on the selected visits.
Excluded outcomes
The workbook keeps 9,392 Out of Business, 7,904 No Entry, 2,735 Not Ready and 28 Business Not Located records. None had an evaluated outcome, so they contribute to neither the numerator nor the denominator.
Category preparation
I standardized capitalization and spacing for common facility types, grouped daycare variants together and labeled the remaining types Other / unspecified. I also grouped re-inspection variants. The workbook keeps the original category fields.
Validation
I matched the CSV’s inspection IDs and retained fields to the source snapshot. Each Inspection ID appeared once during the seven years. I also checked that the yearly, risk and inspection-context totals matched the overall counts.

Interpretation limits

What an inspection rate can tell you

  • A facility can appear several times. The rate tells you how often evaluated visits ended in failure. It cannot tell you the odds that a randomly chosen establishment will fail.
  • Complaints, licensing rules and re-inspection schedules affect which establishments get inspected and how often. A year-to-year change can reflect a different mix of facilities.
  • Of the evaluated inspections, 381 lacked coordinates. I placed each ZIP at its average recorded location across all seven years, so a visit with a usable ZIP could still count there. In all, 79 evaluated visits could not be mapped. They still count in the citywide totals.
  • Each circle summarizes a ZIP code. It does not mark an individual establishment or trace an official ZIP boundary. I kept the city and ZIP labels as recorded, including inconsistencies.
  • Only two evaluated inspections had an unspecified risk level. Both passed. They count in the overall totals but do not appear in the risk chart. Risk level is the city’s classification of the facility.
  • One failure can move the rate considerably in a small group. Check the visit counts and the scale when comparing views. Filtering can change the time axes and map color range, and the establishment-count panels have separate scales.

To investigate the drop further, I’d compare routine inspections within the same facility type and risk level, then look at which establishments received repeat visits.
